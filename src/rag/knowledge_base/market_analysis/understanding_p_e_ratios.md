---
title: Understanding P/E Ratios
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
The Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio divides a company's current stock price
by its earnings per share (EPS), giving a rough sense of how much investors
are paying for each dollar of the company's profit. A P/E of 25 means
investors are paying $25 for every $1 of annual earnings.

Higher P/E ratios often reflect expectations of faster future growth (or,
alternatively, overvaluation), while lower P/E ratios can indicate either
an undervalued stock or genuine concerns about the company's prospects.
P/E ratios are most meaningful when compared to a company's own historical
average, its direct competitors, or its sector average, rather than in
isolation — a "high" P/E in one industry (e.g., fast-growing tech) may be
entirely normal, while the same number would be unusually high in a
slower-growth industry like utilities.

Limitations: P/E isn't meaningful for companies with negative or near-zero
earnings, and it doesn't account for debt levels, cash reserves, or growth
trajectory on its own.
