---
title: What Moves Stock Prices?
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Stock prices are fundamentally driven by supply and demand for shares, but
that demand is shaped by many underlying factors: company-specific news
(earnings reports, product launches, leadership changes, lawsuits),
industry trends, macroeconomic conditions (interest rates, inflation,
employment data), investor sentiment and market psychology, and broader
events (geopolitical developments, regulatory changes).

In the short term, prices can be volatile and driven heavily by sentiment,
speculation, and news flow — sometimes disconnected from a company's
underlying fundamentals. Over the long term, academic research generally
finds that stock prices tend to track a company's earnings growth and cash
flow generation more closely.

This distinction is why many long-term investors focus on company
fundamentals and broad diversification rather than trying to predict or
react to short-term price swings, which are notoriously difficult to
forecast consistently.
