---
title: Sector Rotation Explained
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Sector rotation refers to the tendency of different market sectors
(technology, healthcare, energy, financials, utilities, etc.) to
outperform or underperform at different points in the economic cycle, as
investors shift capital based on changing conditions.

For example, cyclical sectors like technology and consumer discretionary
often perform well during economic expansion when consumer spending and
business investment are strong. Defensive sectors like utilities, consumer
staples, and healthcare tend to hold up relatively better during economic
slowdowns, since demand for their products/services (electricity, food,
medicine) is less sensitive to the economic cycle.

Some active strategies attempt to anticipate and capitalize on these
rotations, but timing sector shifts accurately and consistently is
difficult. Broadly diversified index investors are automatically exposed
to sector rotation effects without needing to predict them, since sector
weights shift naturally within a total-market index over time.
