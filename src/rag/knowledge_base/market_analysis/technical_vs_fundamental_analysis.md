---
title: Technical vs Fundamental Analysis
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Fundamental analysis evaluates a company's intrinsic value by examining
financial statements, competitive position, management quality, industry
trends, and macroeconomic factors — the goal is estimating what a company
is "really worth" versus its current market price.

Technical analysis instead studies historical price and volume patterns,
using charts and indicators (moving averages, support/resistance levels,
momentum indicators) to identify trends and predict likely future price
movement, largely independent of the underlying business fundamentals.

Neither approach is universally "correct" — long-term investors more often
lean on fundamental analysis to select quality companies at reasonable
valuations, while short-term traders more often use technical analysis to
time entries and exits. Some practitioners blend both approaches: using
fundamentals to decide what to buy, and technicals to help decide when.
