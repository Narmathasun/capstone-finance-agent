---
title: What is Beta in Investing?
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Beta measures a stock's (or fund's) volatility relative to the overall
market, typically benchmarked against a broad index like the S&P 500
(which has a beta of 1.0 by definition).

A stock with a beta of 1.5 has historically moved about 50% more than the
market in either direction — larger gains in up markets, larger losses in
down markets. A beta of 0.5 suggests roughly half the market's volatility.
A beta below 0 (rare) would indicate the asset tends to move opposite to
the market.

Beta is a backward-looking statistical measure and doesn't predict future
behavior with certainty — a company's risk profile can change due to new
products, leadership, debt levels, or industry shifts. It's most useful as
one input among several when assessing how a particular holding might
affect a portfolio's overall volatility, not as a standalone risk score.
