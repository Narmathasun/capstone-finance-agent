---
title: What is Market Volatility (VIX)?
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Market volatility refers to how much and how quickly asset prices move,
in either direction. The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), often called the
"fear index," estimates expected S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days
based on options market pricing.

The VIX typically ranges from the low teens during calm markets to 30+ or
higher during periods of significant uncertainty or stress (e.g., major
economic shocks). A rising VIX generally signals increased investor
anxiety and expected price swings, though it measures expected volatility
in either direction, not just declines.

Volatility itself is neither inherently good nor bad — it's a measure of
uncertainty and price movement magnitude, not a direct measure of
long-term investment quality. However, sustained high volatility periods
often coincide with broader market or economic stress, which is why the
VIX is closely watched as a general market-sentiment indicator.
