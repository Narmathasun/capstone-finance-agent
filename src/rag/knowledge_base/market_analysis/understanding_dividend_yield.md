---
title: Understanding Dividend Yield
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Dividend yield is the annual dividend payment per share divided by the
current stock price, expressed as a percentage. It represents the cash
income return an investor receives, separate from any price appreciation.

A high dividend yield isn't automatically a positive signal — it can result
either from a genuinely strong, stable cash-generating business, or from a
falling stock price (since yield rises mathematically as price falls),
sometimes signaling market concern about the company's ability to sustain
the dividend ("yield trap"). It's important to check the "payout ratio"
(dividends paid as a percentage of earnings) — a payout ratio consistently
above 100% suggests a company may be paying out more than it earns, which
is often unsustainable long-term.

Dividend-focused investing is a common strategy for income-oriented
investors, particularly retirees seeking cash flow, but total return
(price appreciation plus dividends) is the more complete measure of
investment performance.
