---
title: Understanding Earnings Reports
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Publicly traded companies report financial results quarterly, disclosing
revenue, profit (or loss), earnings per share (EPS), and forward guidance
for future performance. These earnings reports are among the most
significant scheduled events that can move a stock's price.

Markets react not just to whether a company was profitable, but to whether
results beat or missed analyst expectations ("consensus estimates") — a
company can report record profits and still see its stock fall if results
came in below what was already priced in by investors. Forward guidance
(management's outlook for upcoming quarters) often matters as much as, or
more than, the reported quarter's actual results.

Key metrics investors watch beyond headline revenue/EPS include gross
margin trends, guidance revisions, and commentary on demand trends —
context that a simple "beat or miss" headline doesn't fully capture.
