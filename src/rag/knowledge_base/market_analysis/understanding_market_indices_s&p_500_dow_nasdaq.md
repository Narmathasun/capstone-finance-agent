---
title: Understanding Market Indices (S&P 500, Dow, Nasdaq)
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Market indices track the performance of a defined basket of stocks, serving
as benchmarks for the broader market or specific segments.

The S&P 500 tracks roughly 500 of the largest U.S. companies, weighted by
market capitalization, and is widely considered the best single gauge of
U.S. large-cap stock performance. The Dow Jones Industrial Average tracks
just 30 large, well-established companies and is price-weighted (meaning
higher-priced stocks have more influence, regardless of company size) — a
quirk that makes it a less representative gauge than the S&P 500 despite
its cultural prominence. The Nasdaq Composite tracks all companies listed
on the Nasdaq exchange, which skews heavily toward technology companies.

Understanding which index a fund or comparison references matters — "the
market was up today" typically refers to one of these, and they don't
always move in the same direction on a given day due to their different
compositions.
