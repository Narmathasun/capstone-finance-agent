---
title: Reading a Candlestick Chart
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: market_analysis
---
Candlestick charts are a common way to visualize price movement over a
given time period (a day, an hour, a week, etc.). Each "candle" shows four
data points: the opening price, closing price, and the high/low reached
during that period.

The "body" of the candle spans from the open to the close — typically
colored green/white if the close was higher than the open (price rose) and
red/black if the close was lower (price fell). Thin lines extending above
and below the body ("wicks" or "shadows") show the full high-low range
reached during the period.

Candlestick patterns (like "doji," "hammer," or "engulfing" patterns) are
used in technical analysis to identify potential trend reversals or
continuations, though their predictive reliability is debated among
practitioners and academics — they're best understood as one input for
short-term trading decisions rather than a guarantee of future price
direction.
