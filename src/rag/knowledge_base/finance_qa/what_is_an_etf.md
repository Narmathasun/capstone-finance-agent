---
title: What is an ETF?
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
An Exchange-Traded Fund (ETF) is a basket of securities — stocks, bonds, or
other assets — that trades on an exchange throughout the day, just like an
individual stock. This is the key difference from traditional mutual funds,
which only price and trade once per day after markets close.

ETFs offer instant diversification (a single S&P 500 ETF gives exposure to
500 companies in one trade), typically lower expense ratios than actively
managed mutual funds, and greater tax efficiency in taxable accounts due to
how shares are created and redeemed. ETFs exist for nearly every asset
class and strategy — broad market indices, specific sectors, bonds,
commodities, and more — making them a flexible tool for building a
diversified portfolio with a small number of holdings.
