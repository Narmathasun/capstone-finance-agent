---
title: Active vs Passive Investing
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
Active investing involves a fund manager (or individual investor) making
specific decisions about which securities to buy and sell, aiming to
outperform a benchmark index. Passive investing simply aims to match a
benchmark's performance by holding the same securities as that index.

The active approach offers the possibility of beating the market and can
adjust to changing conditions, but comes with higher fees and the
well-documented difficulty of consistently outperforming after costs — most
active managers underperform their benchmark over 10+ year periods.

Passive investing offers low costs, broad diversification, and tax
efficiency, but by definition never beats its benchmark (it will always lag
slightly due to fees). Many investors use a "core and satellite" approach —
a passive core for most of the portfolio, with a smaller active allocation
for specific convictions.
