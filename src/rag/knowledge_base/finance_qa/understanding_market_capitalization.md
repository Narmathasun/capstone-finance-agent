title: Understanding Market Capitalization
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
Market capitalization ("market cap") is a company's total value as
determined by the stock market — calculated as share price multiplied by
total shares outstanding. It's used to categorize companies by size:

- Large-cap: typically over $10 billion — generally more established,
  stable companies (e.g., large index constituents)
- Mid-cap: roughly $2-10 billion — often growth-stage companies
- Small-cap: roughly $300 million-$2 billion — higher growth potential but
  more volatility and risk

Market cap is not the same as a company's total assets, revenue, or debt —
it only reflects what the market currently thinks the equity is worth,
which can change daily. Many portfolios diversify across cap sizes since
small-caps and large-caps often perform differently across economic cycles.
