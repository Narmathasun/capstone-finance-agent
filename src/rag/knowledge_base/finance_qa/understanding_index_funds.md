An index fund is a type of mutual fund or ETF designed to track the
performance of a specific market index, such as the S&P 500, rather than
having a manager pick individual stocks. Because the fund simply holds the
same securities as the index in the same proportions, it requires far less
active management.

This "passive" approach typically results in much lower fees (often
0.03%-0.20% annually) compared to actively managed funds (often 0.5%-1.5%
or more). Decades of data show that most actively managed funds fail to
beat their benchmark index over long periods, after fees are accounted for
— which is a core reason index funds have become the default building
block for many long-term portfolios, including retirement accounts.
