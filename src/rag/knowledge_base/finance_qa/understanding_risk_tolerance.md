---
title: Understanding Risk Tolerance
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
Risk tolerance is an investor's psychological and financial capacity to
withstand investment losses without abandoning their strategy. It has two
components: risk capacity (how much loss you can financially afford, based
on time horizon, income stability, and goals) and risk appetite (how much
volatility you can emotionally handle without panic-selling).

A common mistake is overestimating risk tolerance during calm markets and
discovering the true limit during a downturn — panic-selling near a market
bottom locks in losses and is one of the most damaging behavioral errors in
investing. Financial questionnaires and time-horizon-based rules of thumb
(e.g., more stock allocation for longer time horizons) are common starting
points, but honest self-assessment matters more than any formula.
