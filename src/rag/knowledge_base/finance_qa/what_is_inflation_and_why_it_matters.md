---
title: What is Inflation and Why It Matters
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
Inflation is the rate at which the general price level of goods and
services rises over time, eroding the purchasing power of money. A dollar
today typically buys less than that same dollar bought ten years ago.

For investors, inflation matters because cash sitting idle loses real value
over time — if inflation runs at 3% annually and your savings account
pays 0.5%, you're losing purchasing power even though the account balance
grows. This is a central reason many financial plans recommend investing
excess cash rather than holding it all in low-yield savings.

Inflation is typically measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI) in the
U.S. Central banks (like the Federal Reserve) actively manage interest
rates partly to keep inflation near a target level, commonly around 2%
annually, balancing price stability against economic growth.
