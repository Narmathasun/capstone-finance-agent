---
title: Why Diversification Matters
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
Diversification means spreading investments across different asset classes,
sectors, and geographies so that a poor outcome in any single holding does
not derail the overall portfolio. The core idea rests on the fact that
different assets often do not move in perfect lockstep — when one zigs,
another may zag — which reduces overall portfolio volatility without
necessarily reducing expected long-term return.

A commonly cited rule of thumb is that concentrating more than roughly
10-15% of a portfolio in a single stock materially increases idiosyncratic
(single-company) risk. Broad index funds are a simple way to achieve
instant diversification across hundreds or thousands of companies in a
single trade.

Diversification does not eliminate market risk (the risk that the entire
market falls), but it substantially reduces company-specific and sector-
specific risk.
