---
title: Stocks vs Bonds: Key Differences
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
Stocks represent partial ownership in a company. When you buy a share, you
own a small slice of that business and benefit if it grows in value or pays
dividends, but you also bear the risk if the company underperforms.

Bonds are loans you make to a company or government. In exchange, the
issuer promises to pay you periodic interest (the coupon) and return your
principal at maturity. Bonds are generally considered lower-risk than
stocks because bondholders are paid before stockholders in bankruptcy, and
bond prices are typically less volatile.

The tradeoff: stocks have historically delivered higher long-term returns
but with more volatility, while bonds offer more predictable income with
lower expected returns. Most diversified portfolios hold a mix of both,
with the ratio often shifting toward bonds as an investor nears retirement.
