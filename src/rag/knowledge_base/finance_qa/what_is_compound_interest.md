---
title: What is Compound Interest?
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal and
the accumulated interest from previous periods. This "interest on interest"
effect means money grows faster over time compared to simple interest, which
only accrues on the original principal.

For example, $10,000 invested at a 7% annual return grows to about $19,672
after 10 years with compounding, versus $17,000 with simple interest. The
longer the time horizon, the more dramatic the difference — this is why
starting to invest early, even with small amounts, is one of the most
repeated pieces of financial advice. Compounding applies to both investment
growth and debt (credit card interest compounds too, which is why
high-interest debt grows quickly if unpaid).
