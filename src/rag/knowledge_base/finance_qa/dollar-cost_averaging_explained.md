---
title: Dollar-Cost Averaging Explained
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed dollar amount at regular
intervals (e.g., $500 every month) regardless of the asset's price, rather
than trying to invest a lump sum at the "perfect" moment.

Because the fixed amount buys more shares when prices are low and fewer
shares when prices are high, DCA naturally averages your purchase price
over time and removes the emotional pressure of trying to time the market.
It's especially useful for investors contributing from regular paychecks
(like 401k contributions), since it happens automatically.

DCA doesn't guarantee better returns than investing a lump sum immediately
— research shows lump-sum investing outperforms DCA more often than not in
rising markets — but DCA reduces regret risk and is psychologically easier
for many investors to stick with consistently.
