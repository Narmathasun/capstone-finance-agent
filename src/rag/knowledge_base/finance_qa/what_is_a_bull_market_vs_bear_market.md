---
title: What is a Bull Market vs Bear Market?
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: finance_qa
---
A bull market refers to a sustained period of rising asset prices, typically
defined as a 20%+ increase from a recent low, often accompanied by investor
optimism and strong economic conditions. A bear market is the opposite — a
sustained decline of 20%+ from a recent high, usually accompanied by
pessimism and often (but not always) an economic slowdown.

Historically, bull markets have lasted much longer on average than bear
markets, and markets have trended upward over multi-decade periods despite
periodic bear markets. This is a key reason long-term investors are
generally advised to stay invested through downturns rather than trying to
exit and re-enter at the "right" time — missing even a handful of the
market's best days (which often cluster near the worst days) can
significantly reduce long-term returns.
