title: Asset Allocation Basics
source: Internal Financial Education Team

Asset allocation refers to how a portfolio is divided among broad asset
classes — typically stocks, bonds, and cash (with real estate, commodities,
or alternatives sometimes included). Research consistently shows that asset
allocation, more than individual security selection, is the primary driver
of a portfolio's long-term risk and return characteristics.

A common starting heuristic is age-based: "110 minus your age" as a rough
stock allocation percentage (e.g., a 30-year-old might target ~80% stocks,
20% bonds), shifting toward more conservative allocations with age.
Target-date retirement funds automate this glide path.

The "right" allocation ultimately depends on time horizon, risk tolerance,
and specific goals rather than a single formula — a 30-year-old saving for
retirement and a 30-year-old saving for a house down payment next year
should likely have very different allocations despite being the same age.
