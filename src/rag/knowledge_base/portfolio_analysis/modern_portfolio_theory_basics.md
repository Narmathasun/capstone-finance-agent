Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT), developed by economist Harry Markowitz,
formalized the idea that a portfolio's overall risk depends not just on
the risk of individual holdings but on how those holdings move in relation
to each other (their correlation). Combining assets that don't move in
perfect lockstep can reduce overall portfolio volatility without
necessarily sacrificing expected return — the mathematical basis for
diversification.

MPT introduced the concept of the "efficient frontier" — the set of
portfolios offering the highest expected return for a given level of risk
(or the lowest risk for a given expected return). Portfolios below the
frontier are considered suboptimal, since a different combination of
assets could achieve better risk-adjusted results.

While MPT has real limitations (it relies on historical data and
assumptions about how returns are distributed, which don't always hold in
real markets, especially during crises when correlations often rise
together), it remains foundational to how diversification and asset
allocation are taught and applied today.
