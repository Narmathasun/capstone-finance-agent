---
title: Understanding Portfolio Rebalancing
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: portfolio_analysis
---
Rebalancing is the process of periodically buying or selling holdings to
bring a portfolio back to its target asset allocation, since different
assets grow at different rates over time and drift from original targets.
For example, if stocks significantly outperform bonds over a year, a
portfolio originally set at 70% stocks/30% bonds might drift to 78%/22%,
increasing risk beyond the investor's intended level.

Common rebalancing approaches include calendar-based (e.g., rebalancing
every 6 or 12 months) and threshold-based (rebalancing whenever an
allocation drifts more than a set percentage, e.g., 5%, from target).

Rebalancing has a useful side effect: it systematically enforces "sell
high, buy low" discipline, since it involves trimming the assets that have
grown the most and adding to those that have lagged. In tax-advantaged
accounts this can be done without tax consequences; in taxable accounts,
using new contributions to rebalance (rather than selling) can help
minimize triggered capital gains.
