---
title: Understanding Correlation Between Assets
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: portfolio_analysis
---
Correlation measures how closely two assets' prices move together, on a
scale from -1 to +1. A correlation of +1 means the assets move in perfect
lockstep; -1 means they move in exactly opposite directions; 0 means no
consistent relationship.

Diversification benefits come from combining assets with low or negative
correlation — since a low-correlation asset is more likely to hold steady
or gain when another asset in the portfolio declines, smoothing overall
portfolio volatility. Stocks and bonds have historically shown relatively
low (sometimes negative) correlation, which is a core reason many
portfolios include both.

A key risk: correlations aren't fixed — many assets that appear
uncorrelated in normal markets can become highly correlated during a
severe crisis, as investors sell broadly across asset classes for
liquidity. This is why relying purely on historical correlation data has
real limitations during extreme market events.
