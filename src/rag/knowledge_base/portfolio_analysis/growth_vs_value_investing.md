---
title: Growth vs Value Investing
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: portfolio_analysis
---
Growth investing focuses on companies expected to grow revenue and earnings
faster than the broader market, often reinvesting profits back into the
business rather than paying dividends. Growth stocks typically trade at
higher valuation multiples (like P/E ratio) reflecting expectations of
future expansion, and are often concentrated in sectors like technology.

Value investing focuses on companies trading at a discount relative to
their underlying fundamentals — often measured by lower P/E, price-to-book,
or other valuation ratios — betting that the market has temporarily
underpriced the business relative to its true worth.

Historically, both styles have gone through extended periods of
outperforming the other, and predicting which style will lead going
forward is difficult. Many diversified portfolios hold both growth and
value exposure (often via separate index funds, or simply through a total
market index that naturally includes both) rather than betting heavily on
one style over the other.
