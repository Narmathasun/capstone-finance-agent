---
title: Home Bias in Investing
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: portfolio_analysis
---
Home bias refers to the well-documented tendency of investors to hold a
disproportionately large share of their portfolio in domestic (home
country) investments relative to that country's actual share of global
market capitalization. U.S. investors, for example, often hold far more
than the U.S.'s roughly 60-65% share of global stock market value would
suggest is "neutral."

Home bias arises from familiarity, currency considerations, and
behavioral comfort with domestic companies and news coverage — but it can
reduce diversification benefits, since international markets don't always
move in lockstep with domestic markets, and can concentrate risk in a
single country's economic and political conditions.

Many financial professionals suggest maintaining meaningful international
exposure (commonly cited ranges vary from 20% to 40%+ of total stock
allocation) as part of a broadly diversified portfolio, though the exact
right allocation is debated and depends on individual circumstances.
