title: Tax-Efficient Fund Placement
source: Internal Financial Education Team

Tax-efficient fund placement (or "asset location," distinct from "asset
allocation") is the practice of strategically placing different types of
investments in different account types — taxable brokerage, Traditional
tax-deferred, and Roth tax-free — based on each investment's tax
characteristics, to minimize overall tax drag.

General guidelines: tax-inefficient investments that generate significant
ordinary income (like actively managed bond funds or REITs, which
distribute income taxed at ordinary rates) are often better held in
tax-advantaged accounts. Tax-efficient investments (broad index funds and
ETFs, which generate relatively little taxable distribution activity) are
often reasonable to hold in taxable accounts. High-growth-potential assets
are sometimes prioritized for Roth accounts, since all future growth in a
Roth is tax-free.

This is a nuanced, personalized area — the "correct" placement depends on
overall account balances, time horizons, and expected future tax
circumstances, making it worth consulting a tax professional for
significant portfolios.
