---
title: Concentration Risk Explained
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: portfolio_analysis
---
Concentration risk occurs when a large portion of a portfolio's value is
tied to a single stock, sector, or asset — meaning that one investment's
poor performance can significantly damage overall portfolio returns.

Common sources of unintended concentration include: holding a large amount
of employer stock (common with stock compensation or ESPPs), letting a
single winning stock grow to dominate a portfolio through appreciation
alone, or having significant overlap between separate fund holdings that
each hold many of the same underlying companies.

While concentrated positions can lead to outsized gains if that holding
performs well, they also expose the investor to outsized, company-specific
risk that diversification is specifically designed to reduce. Many
financial professionals suggest reviewing whether any single position
exceeds roughly 10-15% of total portfolio value, and considering a plan to
gradually reduce concentration (mindful of tax consequences) if it does.
