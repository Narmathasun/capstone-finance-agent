title: Understanding Tax Brackets
source: Internal Financial Education Team

The U.S. uses a progressive, marginal tax bracket system — meaning
different portions of your income are taxed at different rates, not your
entire income at one flat rate. As of 2025, federal brackets range from 10%
to 37%.

A common misconception: moving into a higher tax bracket does NOT mean all
your income gets taxed at the higher rate. Only the income that falls
within that bracket's range is taxed at that rate. For example, if the 22%
bracket starts at $47,150, only income above that threshold is taxed at
22% — income below it is still taxed at the lower 10% and 12% rates that
apply to those portions.

This is why "your effective tax rate" (total tax paid divided by total
income) is always lower than your "marginal tax rate" (the rate on your
last dollar earned) — an important distinction for financial planning.
