---
title: 401(k) Plans Explained
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: tax_education
---
A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement plan that lets employees
contribute a portion of their paycheck pre-tax (Traditional 401k) or
after-tax (Roth 401k), with investment growth tax-deferred or tax-free
respectively. Many employers offer a matching contribution — for example,
matching 50% of employee contributions up to 6% of salary — which is
effectively free money and generally worth maximizing before contributing
elsewhere.

For 2025, the employee contribution limit is $23,500 ($31,000 if 50+, with
an additional "catch-up" provision for ages 60-63). Unlike IRAs, 401(k)
plans have employer-selected investment menus, usually a limited set of
mutual funds or target-date funds rather than unlimited stock/ETF choices.
Funds can generally be rolled over into an IRA when leaving an employer,
which often opens up a wider range of investment options.
