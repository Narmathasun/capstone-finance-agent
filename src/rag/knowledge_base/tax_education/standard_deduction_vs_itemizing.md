---
title: Standard Deduction vs Itemizing
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: tax_education
---
When filing taxes, you reduce your taxable income either by taking the
standard deduction (a fixed dollar amount set by the IRS each year) or by
itemizing specific deductible expenses (mortgage interest, state and local
taxes up to a cap, charitable donations, certain medical expenses above a
threshold, etc.) — whichever produces a larger deduction.

For 2025, the standard deduction is $15,000 for single filers and $30,000
for married filing jointly. Since the standard deduction roughly doubled
under 2017 tax law changes, the majority of U.S. taxpayers now take the
standard deduction rather than itemizing, since their itemizable expenses
don't exceed it. Itemizing tends to make sense primarily for homeowners
with large mortgage interest payments or those with substantial charitable
giving or high state/local tax bills.
