---
title: Understanding Marriage and Tax Filing Status
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: tax_education
---
Filing status significantly affects your tax brackets, standard deduction,
and eligibility for various credits and deductions. The main options are
Single, Married Filing Jointly, Married Filing Separately, and Head of
Household.

Married Filing Jointly generally offers the most favorable brackets and
the largest standard deduction, which is why most married couples choose
it — but it also means both spouses are jointly liable for the full tax
bill, including any errors or omissions. Married Filing Separately is
typically only advantageous in specific situations, such as when one
spouse has significant medical expenses relative to income, or concerns
about liability for a spouse's tax reporting.

Head of Household status offers better brackets than Single for unmarried
individuals who pay more than half the cost of maintaining a home for a
qualifying dependent — often overlooked by single parents who qualify.
