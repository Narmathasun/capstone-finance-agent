---
title: Understanding Capital Gains Tax
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: tax_education
---
Capital gains tax applies to the profit from selling an investment for more
than you paid for it. The rate depends on how long you held the asset:

- Short-term capital gains (held one year or less) are taxed at your
  ordinary income tax rate, which can be significantly higher.
- Long-term capital gains (held more than one year) benefit from
  preferential rates — 0%, 15%, or 20% for 2025, depending on income.

This difference creates a strong incentive to hold appreciated investments
for at least a year before selling when possible. Capital losses can offset
capital gains, and up to $3,000 of net losses can offset ordinary income
per year, with excess losses carried forward to future years — a strategy
known as tax-loss harvesting.
