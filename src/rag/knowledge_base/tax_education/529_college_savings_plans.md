---
title: 529 College Savings Plans
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: tax_education
---
A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged account designed for education savings.
Contributions grow tax-free, and withdrawals are tax-free when used for
qualified education expenses — tuition, room and board, books, and (as of
recent law changes) certain K-12 tuition and student loan repayment up to
limits.

Contributions aren't federally tax-deductible, but many states offer a
state income tax deduction or credit for contributions to their own state's
plan. There's no federal annual contribution limit, though contributions
are treated as gifts for gift-tax purposes (with special rules allowing
five years of gift-tax-exclusion contributions in a single year).

If a beneficiary doesn't use all the funds for education, unused 529 funds
can now be rolled over (subject to lifetime limits and account-age rules)
into a Roth IRA for the beneficiary — a relatively recent change that
reduced the "wasted savings" risk that previously discouraged some
families from contributing generously.
