title: Understanding Economic Indicators (CPI, GDP, Unemployment)

Economic indicators are regularly released government/institutional data
points that markets watch closely for signs of economic health and future
policy direction.

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures the average change in prices paid
by consumers for a basket of goods and services over time — the primary
gauge of inflation. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) measures the total value
of goods and services produced in the economy, typically reported
quarterly, and is the broadest single measure of economic growth (or
contraction). The unemployment rate and monthly jobs report measure labor
market health, which the Fed weighs alongside inflation when setting policy.

Markets often react sharply to these releases, especially when actual
figures differ meaningfully from economist consensus forecasts — a
"stronger than expected" jobs report, for instance, can sometimes cause
stocks to fall if it raises expectations that the Fed will keep interest
rates higher for longer to control inflation.
