---
title: Understanding Fed Rate Decisions and Market Impact
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: news_synthesizer
---
The Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank) sets the federal funds rate — a
benchmark interest rate that influences borrowing costs throughout the
economy, from mortgages to credit cards to business loans. The Fed adjusts
this rate primarily to manage inflation and support stable employment,
raising rates to cool an overheating economy/inflation and lowering rates
to stimulate a slowing economy.

Markets often react not just to the rate decision itself, but to the Fed's
accompanying commentary and forward guidance about future policy
intentions — sometimes a rate change that was already widely expected
causes little market reaction, while unexpected commentary about future
plans moves markets significantly ("the market had already priced it in").

Higher rates generally increase borrowing costs for companies (potentially
pressuring stock valuations, especially for growth companies reliant on
future earnings) and tend to make bonds more attractive relative to their
recent yields, while lower rates tend to have the opposite effects.
