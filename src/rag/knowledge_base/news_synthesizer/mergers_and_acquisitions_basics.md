---
title: Mergers and Acquisitions Basics
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: news_synthesizer
---
A merger occurs when two companies combine into a single new entity; an
acquisition occurs when one company purchases and absorbs another
(the acquired company's stock typically stops trading once the deal
closes). M&A news can significantly move stock prices — the target
company's stock often jumps toward the offered acquisition price when a
deal is announced, while the acquiring company's stock reaction varies
based on market perception of whether the deal is a good use of capital.

Deals typically require regulatory approval (e.g., from antitrust
authorities) before closing, which can take months, and deals sometimes
fall through if regulators block them or shareholders reject the terms —
which is why an announced deal's target-company stock price often trades
somewhat below the announced acquisition price until the deal is confirmed
closed, reflecting deal-completion risk ("merger arbitrage" spread).

Understanding whether a reported "deal" is a signed definitive agreement,
a preliminary offer, or just rumored speculation is important context
often buried below a dramatic headline.
