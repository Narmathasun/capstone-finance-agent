---
title: What is Market Sentiment?
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: news_synthesizer
---
Market sentiment refers to the overall attitude of investors toward a
particular security or the market as a whole — whether optimistic
("bullish") or pessimistic ("bearish") — which can influence prices
independently of, or in addition to, underlying fundamentals.

Sentiment is often gauged through indicators like the VIX (volatility
index), surveys of investor confidence, put/call options ratios, and
social media/news sentiment analysis. Extreme sentiment readings in either
direction are sometimes viewed as contrarian signals by some investors —
extreme euphoria has historically preceded some market pullbacks, while
extreme pessimism has sometimes preceded recoveries — though this pattern
is far from a reliable, consistent predictor.

Sentiment can drive short-term price swings that diverge meaningfully from
a company's or market's underlying fundamentals, which is part of why
short-term price movement is generally considered far less predictable
than long-term trends tied to fundamentals.
