---
title: Understanding Analyst Ratings and Price Targets
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: news_synthesizer
---
Wall Street analysts at brokerage and research firms issue ratings (Buy,
Hold, Sell, or similar variants) and price targets for individual stocks,
based on their own financial models and research. These ratings are widely
reported in financial news and can influence short-term stock price
movement, particularly when a rating changes.

It's important to understand these are opinions, not guarantees — studies
of analyst price target accuracy show mixed results, and individual
analysts can have conflicting views on the same stock at the same time.
Analysts may also have potential conflicts of interest if their firm has
other business relationships with the company being covered (investment
banking fees, for example), though disclosure rules require firms to
flag such relationships.

News often reports the "average" or "consensus" price target across many
analysts, which can be a useful sentiment gauge, but shouldn't be treated
as a reliable prediction of where a stock's price will actually go.
