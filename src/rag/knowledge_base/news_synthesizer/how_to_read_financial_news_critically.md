---
title: How to Read Financial News Critically
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: news_synthesizer
---
Financial news headlines are often written to maximize clicks and can
emphasize short-term drama over substantive context. A stock "plunging 3%"
sounds dramatic in a headline, but may be well within normal daily
volatility for that stock and warrant little concern for a long-term
investor.

Useful questions to ask when reading financial news: Is this move large
relative to the asset's normal volatility, or within a typical daily
range? Is the article describing an isolated event or part of a broader
trend across the sector/market? Does the article distinguish between an
analyst's opinion/prediction and confirmed facts? Who benefits from this
story being framed a particular way (e.g., sponsored content, a firm
promoting its own trading products)?

Cross-referencing a notable story across multiple independent, reputable
outlets — and being skeptical of unsourced claims or purely speculative
predictions — helps build a more balanced picture than reacting to any
single headline in isolation.
