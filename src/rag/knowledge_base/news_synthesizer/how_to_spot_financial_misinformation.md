---
title: How to Spot Financial Misinformation
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: news_synthesizer
---
Financial misinformation spreads easily, particularly on social media,
where guaranteed-return claims, "insider" stock tips, and pump-and-dump
schemes can circulate rapidly. Several warning signs are worth watching
for: claims of guaranteed or unusually high returns with "no risk" (all
legitimate investments carry some risk), urgency-based pressure to act
immediately, unverifiable claims of insider knowledge, and sources with
financial incentive to promote a specific stock without disclosing that
incentive.

Reliable financial information typically comes from regulated sources:
company SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K reports), established financial news
outlets with editorial standards, and licensed financial professionals.
Cross-checking surprising claims against multiple independent, reputable
sources before acting is a simple but effective habit.

Regulators like the SEC and FINRA publish investor-alert resources
specifically warning about common current scam patterns, which are worth
periodically reviewing, since scam tactics continually evolve alongside
new platforms and trends (e.g., cryptocurrency-related scams, AI-themed
investment fraud).
