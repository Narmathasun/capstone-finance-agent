---
title: Understanding Net Worth and Why It Matters
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning
---
Net worth is calculated as total assets (cash, investments, retirement
accounts, real estate, vehicles) minus total liabilities (mortgage, credit
card debt, student loans, auto loans). It's a single number that summarizes
overall financial position better than income alone — a high earner with
heavy debt and no savings may have a lower net worth than a modest earner
who has saved consistently.

Tracking net worth over time (e.g., quarterly or annually) is a useful way
to measure financial progress toward long-term goals, independent of
short-term market noise in any single account. A rising net worth trend
over multiple years — even through periods where investments decline in
value — generally indicates healthy financial habits (saving more than
spending, paying down debt).

Many people find it motivating to track net worth as their primary
financial "scoreboard," rather than fixating on the daily fluctuations of
individual investment accounts.
