---
title: Saving for a Home Down Payment
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning
---
A down payment is the upfront cash portion of a home purchase, with the
remainder financed via a mortgage. Conventional loans often require 20%
down to avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI), though many programs allow
much lower down payments (as low as 3-3.5% for certain first-time-buyer or
FHA loans), with PMI added to monthly costs until sufficient equity is
built.

Because a home down payment is typically needed within a defined,
relatively short timeframe (1-5 years), this savings goal is usually kept
in lower-volatility vehicles — high-yield savings accounts, CDs, or
short-term bonds — rather than stocks, since a market downturn right before
a planned purchase could force selling at a loss.

Beyond the down payment, buyers should budget for closing costs (typically
2-5% of the loan amount), moving expenses, and an emergency reserve for
home repairs, which are easy to underestimate when focused solely on the
down payment target.
