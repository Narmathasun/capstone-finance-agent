---
title: Planning for College Costs
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning
---
College cost planning typically balances savings vehicles (like 529 plans),
financial aid, scholarships, and student loans. Because tuition costs have
historically risen faster than general inflation, starting to save early —
even small amounts — meaningfully reduces the eventual funding gap.

A useful planning exercise: estimate the target school's total cost
(tuition, room, board) inflated to the expected enrollment year, decide
what portion the family aims to cover through savings versus loans/aid, and
work backward to a monthly savings target using an estimated investment
return.

It's worth noting that retirement savings should generally not be
sacrificed to fully fund college — students have access to loans and
financial aid, but there's no equivalent borrowing mechanism for
retirement. Many financial planners recommend prioritizing retirement
contributions (at least up to any employer match) before maximizing
college savings.
