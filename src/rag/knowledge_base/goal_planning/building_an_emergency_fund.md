---
title: Building an Emergency Fund
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning
---
An emergency fund is cash set aside specifically to cover unexpected
expenses — job loss, medical bills, urgent home or car repairs — without
having to sell investments at a bad time or rely on high-interest debt.

A common guideline is 3-6 months of essential living expenses, though the
right amount varies: those with unstable income (freelancers, commission-
based roles) or a single-income household often benefit from 6-12 months,
while dual-income households with very stable jobs may be comfortable
closer to 3 months.

Emergency funds are typically kept in a high-yield savings account —
liquid and safe, but earning some interest — rather than invested in
stocks, since the money may be needed on short notice when markets could
be down. Building this fund is generally recommended as a first financial
priority, before aggressive investing, since it prevents debt spirals when
life's inevitable surprises happen.
