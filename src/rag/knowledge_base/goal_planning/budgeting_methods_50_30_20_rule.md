---
title: Budgeting Methods: 50/30/20 Rule
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning
---
The 50/30/20 rule is a simple budgeting framework that allocates after-tax
income into three broad categories: 50% for needs (housing, utilities,
groceries, minimum debt payments), 30% for wants (dining out,
entertainment, subscriptions), and 20% for savings and extra debt paydown.

This is a starting heuristic rather than a rigid rule — someone in a
high-cost-of-living area may need to allocate well over 50% to needs, while
someone with an aggressive savings goal might push the savings category
higher at the expense of wants.

Other popular budgeting approaches include zero-based budgeting (every
dollar is assigned a purpose until income minus expenses equals zero) and
the envelope method (allocating physical or virtual "envelopes" of cash to
spending categories). The right method is largely about which system a
person will actually maintain consistently over time.
