title: Life Insurance Basics
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning

Life insurance provides a payout to beneficiaries if the insured person
dies during the policy's coverage. The two broad categories are term life
and permanent (whole/universal) life insurance.

Term life insurance covers a specific period (e.g., 20 years) and pays out
only if death occurs during that term; it has no cash value and is
significantly cheaper than permanent insurance for the same death benefit,
making it the most common recommendation for pure income-replacement needs
(e.g., replacing a parent's income until children are financially
independent).

Permanent life insurance covers the insured's entire life and includes a
cash-value component that grows over time, but costs considerably more for
equivalent coverage. It's generally suited to specific estate-planning or
tax situations rather than general income replacement. A common rule of
thumb for coverage amount is 10-12x annual income, adjusted for existing
savings, debts, and dependents' needs.
