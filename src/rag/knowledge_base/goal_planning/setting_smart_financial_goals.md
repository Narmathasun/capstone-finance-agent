---
title: Setting SMART Financial Goals
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning
---
SMART is a framework for setting effective goals: Specific, Measurable,
Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. Applied to finances, it turns vague
intentions ("save more money") into actionable plans.

For example, instead of "save for retirement," a SMART version might be:
"Contribute $500/month to my 401(k) to reach $50,000 saved by age 35, by
automating payroll deductions starting next paycheck." This is specific
(exact contribution amount and target), measurable (a dollar goal and
timeline), achievable (based on current income), relevant (tied to a
retirement objective), and time-bound (a clear deadline).

Breaking large financial goals into SMART sub-goals — an emergency fund
target, a debt-payoff date, a specific savings-rate percentage — makes
progress trackable and helps identify early whether a plan is on pace or
needs adjustment.
