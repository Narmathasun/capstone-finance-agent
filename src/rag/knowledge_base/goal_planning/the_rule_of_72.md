---
title: The Rule of 72
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning
---
The Rule of 72 is a quick mental-math shortcut for estimating how long it
takes an investment to double in value at a given annual rate of return.
Simply divide 72 by the annual return percentage.

For example: at a 6% annual return, an investment doubles in roughly 12
years (72 ÷ 6). At 8%, it doubles in about 9 years. At 10%, about 7.2
years. This approximation works reasonably well for rates between about
6% and 10%; it becomes less accurate at very high or very low rates.

The rule is useful for building intuition about how return rate and time
horizon interact — it illustrates why even a few extra percentage points
of return, or a few extra years of compounding, can make a large
difference to a long-term goal like retirement savings.
