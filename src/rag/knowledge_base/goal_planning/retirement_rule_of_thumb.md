---
title: The 4% Rule for Retirement Withdrawals
source: Internal Financial Education Team
category: goal_planning
---
The 4% rule is a widely referenced retirement-planning heuristic suggesting
that a retiree can withdraw 4% of their portfolio's value in the first year
of retirement, then adjust that dollar amount for inflation each subsequent
year, with a high historical probability the portfolio lasts 30 years.

Working backward, this implies a target portfolio of roughly 25 times
annual expenses. For example, someone who needs $60,000/year in retirement
income would target a portfolio of approximately $1,500,000.

The rule is a starting heuristic, not a guarantee — it was derived from
historical U.S. market returns and does not account for sequence-of-returns
risk, changing spending needs, taxes, or non-U.S. market conditions. Many
planners now suggest a more conservative 3-3.5% starting withdrawal rate
given lower expected future returns.
